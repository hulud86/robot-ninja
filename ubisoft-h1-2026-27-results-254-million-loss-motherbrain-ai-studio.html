Ubisoft posts a €254.5 million half-year loss and spins out a generative AI studio

Net bookings fell 16.5% in the six months to September, headcount dropped by about 1,100, and Ubisoft confirmed Motherbrain as a dedicated subsidiary for AI in gameplay.

By Robot Ninja staff, November 21, 2026

Ubisoft's first-half results show a publisher still shrinking while it reorganizes. In the six months to September 30, net bookings came to €644.7 million, down 16.5% from €772.4 million a year earlier. The second quarter alone brought in €388.9 million, a 20.8% drop, though Ubisoft noted that was ahead of its own guidance of around €370 million.

The bottom line got worse. The IFRS net loss attributable to shareholders widened to €254.5 million from €161.3 million, and the IFRS operating loss grew to €189.2 million.

Fewer people, smaller costs

Ubisoft says its headcount fell by close to 1,100 over the half and its fixed cost base dropped 6% to €660 million. The results arrived the same week Ubisoft announced 166 more layoffs in Montreal and Quebec City. It ended September with about €1.03 billion in cash, and says it used cash in mid-November to settle a €453 million convertible bond that holders chose to redeem early. A two-part refinancing plan is underway.

Motherbrain becomes its own company

The report also confirms Motherbrain as a dedicated subsidiary focused on AI for gameplay. CEO Yves Guillemot described it as the team behind Teammates, the experimental generative AI project Ubisoft showed in late 2025, which uses AI-driven characters that respond to players. According to VGC, it is not yet clear whether Motherbrain's work will ship as standalone products or be built into other Ubisoft games. Ubisoft says its new operating model, which began rolling out in January alongside six game cancellations, is now fully in place.

What's next

Ubisoft kept its full-year targets: net bookings down by a high single-digit percentage, a negative non-IFRS operating margin, and free cash outflow of no more than €500 million. It expects around €250 million in third-quarter net bookings, with Rayman Legends Retold and Just Dance: Decades of Hits among this quarter's releases. Rainbow Six Tactics and the Heroes of Might and Magic III remake are slated for 2027.
