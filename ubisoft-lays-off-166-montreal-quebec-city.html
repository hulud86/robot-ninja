Ubisoft cuts 166 jobs at its Montreal and Quebec City studios

The layoffs mainly hit production and support roles tied to cancelled or reworked projects, extending a year of closures and cuts at the Assassin's Creed publisher.

By Robot Ninja staff, November 20, 2026

Ubisoft is laying off 166 people across its Montreal and Quebec City operations, two of its largest studios and the main homes of Assassin's Creed. The company confirmed the cuts on November 20.

A Ubisoft spokesperson said the company's production needs are changing and that it is adjusting team sizes to match current production priorities and business needs. According to GamesIndustry.biz, the affected staff are mainly in production and support roles connected to projects that were recently cancelled or significantly changed. PocketGamer.biz says the cuts amount to less than 4% of headcount at the two studios, and reportedly include staff at Vantage Studios, the Tencent-backed subsidiary that runs Assassin's Creed, Far Cry and Rainbow Six.

A long year of cuts

This is far from the first round in 2026:

  • Early in the year Ubisoft made what it described as a final round of cuts aimed at about 200 million euros in savings.
  • It has since closed Ubisoft Winnipeg, Ubisoft Belgrade and Ubisoft Halifax.
  • Its headcount fell by roughly 1,100 between April and September.
  • In October it announced plans to close Finnish studio RedLynx and cut 70 jobs at Ubisoft Singapore, and said Rainbow Six Mobile will shut down in January 2027.

The timing is notable. The cuts land a day after the biggest game launch in history, a reminder that blockbuster sales elsewhere haven't stopped the industry's wave of layoffs.
